## What Makes Roofing in Bluffton Different?

Bluffton roofs face storm season and a split between very old and very new houses. The town sits about 20 feet above sea level, with the May River running through its center and the New River along its southwest edge. The climate is humid and subtropical, with hot summers, and hurricanes threaten in summer and early fall, so wind-driven rain is the test every roof here has to pass. In Old Town, a National Historic District since 1996, some homes date to the 1800s, before the Civil War. Most of the rest of Bluffton is new: the town grew from 1,275 people in 2000 to 27,716 in 2020, so many Bluffton roofs sit on recently built homes.
