## Which Kinds of Gutter Guards Work on Rain Gutters?

Most gutter guards fit one of four kinds: micro-mesh, screens, hoods, or foam and brush inserts. Each one keeps leaves out in a different way, and each has a weak spot.

Gutter guards come in two broad styles: solid covers that shed debris over a curved edge, and mesh or screen guards that filter it out.

### Micro-Mesh Gutter Guards

A micro-mesh gutter guard is a fine metal mesh held in a frame over the gutter. The holes are small enough to stop leaves, pine needles and most grit. Rain soaks through the mesh. Fine dirt can still sit on top, so the surface needs an occasional brush or rinse.

### Screen Gutter Guards

A screen gutter guard is a sheet of metal or plastic with larger holes, laid over the gutter. It stops big leaves and costs less than mesh. Small debris, like pine needles and seed pods, can slip through the holes or get stuck in them.

### Gutter Hoods

A gutter hood is a solid cover that curves over the front of the gutter. Rain follows the curve and drops in through a narrow gap, while leaves slide off the edge. In a very hard downpour, some water can run past the gap instead of into the gutter.

### Foam and Brush Gutter Inserts

A foam or brush gutter insert sits inside the gutter itself. Foam blocks leaves while water seeps through. A brush insert catches leaves on its bristles. Both are simple to put in, but they trap debris over time and need to come out for cleaning.

### What are the downsides of gutter guards?

The main downsides of gutter guards are the cost up front, the fact that they still need some cleaning, and the risk of overflow in very heavy rain with some styles, and a guard that is fitted badly can trap debris or lift roof shingles. No guard is truly zero-upkeep. A good fit matters as much as the type.

### What is the difference between a leaf guard and a gutter guard?

There is usually no real difference between a leaf guard and a gutter guard, because most people use both names for any cover or insert that keeps leaves out of a gutter and lets rain in. Ask which kind is in your quote.

### What do roofers say about gutter guards?

Roofers mostly worry about how a gutter guard attaches, because a guard slid under the shingles can break their seal or bend them, so check the shingle maker's instructions. Guards that fasten to the gutter itself avoid that problem. At your free inspection, ask exactly how the guard will attach.
