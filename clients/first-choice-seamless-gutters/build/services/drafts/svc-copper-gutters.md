## What Kinds of Copper Gutters and Downspouts Can You Choose From?

Most copper gutter systems come down to two choices. One is the gutter shape, half-round or K-style. The other is the downspouts that carry water to the ground. [NEED: which copper gutter profiles, sizes and downspout styles First Choice installs — client]

### Half-Round Copper Gutters

Half-round copper gutters are shaped like a pipe cut down the middle, with a rolled front edge. It is the traditional shape for copper and suits older homes and homes built in a classic style. For the same width, a half-round gutter holds a little less water than a K-style gutter.

### K-Style Copper Gutters

K-style copper gutters have a flat back that sits against the house and a front with curves like crown molding. It is the most common gutter shape on newer homes. For its width, a K-style gutter carries more water, which helps on roofs that shed heavy rain.

### Copper Downspouts

Copper downspouts carry water from the gutter to the ground and come in round or rectangular shapes to match the gutter. They should be copper too, along with the elbows and straps. Mixing metals where water flows can make the other metal wear out early.

### Can copper and aluminum gutters be mixed?

Copper and aluminum gutters should not be mixed where they touch or share water, because when the two metals meet in rainwater the aluminum wears away much faster, a reaction called galvanic corrosion, and water running off copper onto aluminum or galvanized steel can damage those metals too. ### Should copper gutters be soldered?

Yes, copper gutter seams, corners and end caps are normally soldered, which means a metal filler is melted into each joint to make a tight seal that keeps water in for many years, while caulk alone can dry out, crack and leak. A soldered joint looks clean, too. There is no bead of caulk to see.
