A downspout has three jobs on rain gutters:

1. Drain its length of gutter fast enough that water never spills over the front edge.
2. Carry the water down the wall without leaking at the joints.
3. Let the water out far enough from the house that it soaks in or runs off instead of pooling.

A downspout that is too small, too far from the end of a long run, or that stops right at the wall fails one of those jobs. Water then ends up against the house, where the gutters were meant to keep it away.

### 2x3 and 3x4 Downspout Sizes

Home downspouts come in two common sizes: 2x3 inches and 3x4 inches. The 3x4 has twice the opening of the 2x3, 12 square inches against 6. So it moves more water and is harder to plug with leaves. A bigger downspout suits a long gutter run, a large roof section, or a gutter under trees.

### Downspouts in Aluminum, Copper and Galvalume

A downspout is normally made of the same metal as the gutter it drains. First Choice Seamless Gutters installs aluminum, copper and Galvalume gutters. Matching the metal keeps the look the same from roof edge to ground. It also avoids joining copper to other metals, which can corrode the joint.

### Downspout Elbows, Extensions and Splash Blocks

Downspout elbows bend the pipe at the top, to reach the wall, and at the bottom, to point the water away. An extension carries the water farther out into the yard. A splash block is a small pad under the exit. It spreads the flow so it does not dig into the soil.

### Underground Downspout Drains

An underground downspout drain carries the water in a buried pipe to an exit farther from the house, such as a pop-up outlet in the lawn. It keeps the yard free of extensions you can trip over or hit with a mower. The pipe has to slope downhill the whole way, so water keeps moving.

### Replacing a Damaged or Undersized Downspout

A downspout is worth replacing when it is crushed, split at a seam, pulling off the wall, or too small for a heavy storm. If a gutter overflows at the outlet while its downspout runs full, the fix is often a bigger downspout or a second one. Another cleaning will not solve it.

### What is the rule of thumb for how many downspouts a gutter needs?

The usual rule of thumb is one downspout for every gutter run, plus another when the run is long or drains a big roof. One downspout can only clear so much water. Past that, the gutter spills over its front edge. At the free on-site inspection, First Choice can look at how much roof drains into each of your gutters.

### Can you put a 3x4 downspout on a 5-inch gutter?

Often, yes, when the outlet is cut to the larger size; it depends on the gutter's profile. It drains faster than a 2x3 because its opening is twice as big. Leaves and grit pass through instead of plugging the pipe.

### What can I use instead of a downspout?

The main options instead of a standard downspout are a rain chain and a buried drain. A rain chain guides water down a hanging chain of cups or links. A buried drain takes the water from the gutter outlet underground. Rain chains splash and can overflow in a heavy storm, so they suit a porch better than a main gutter run.

### How much does it cost to replace a downspout?

The cost to replace a downspout depends on its height, its size, its metal and the extras it needs. A 3x4 costs more than a 2x3, and copper costs more than aluminum. Each elbow, extension or buried drain adds to the price. First Choice measures the job at a free on-site inspection and estimate, free even if you don't book.

### Where should downspouts drain on a Florida lot?

On a Florida lot, downspouts should drain onto ground that slopes away from the house, several feet out from the wall. They should never point at the foundation, a neighbor's yard or a septic drain field. Bare soil washes out when water pours on one spot. An extension, a splash block or a buried drain spreads the flow.
