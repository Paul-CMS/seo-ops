# Gap Interview

A short conversation with the account manager after discovery, before the client sees
anything. Eight questions maximum, one batch.

The constraint is the point. If this turns into a twenty-question interrogation, the
workflow hasn't saved anyone time — it has just moved the burden from the client to the
person who was supposed to be freed up by it.

## What qualifies as a question

**1. Conflicts between sources of similar authority.** Where discovery found two
plausible values and picking wrong would put a false claim on the sheet. If one source
is clearly authoritative, don't ask — use it and note the discrepancy on the sheet for
the client.

**2. Things only the sales conversation would have surfaced.** Whether they're dropping
a service line, expanding into a new town, rebranding, changing the phone number,
opening a second location, splitting from a partner. None of this is public yet and all
of it changes what gets built.

**3. Scope decisions that change the sheet's structure.** Which MSA is primary. Whether
this is a one-location or multi-location build. Whether the engagement includes the
team page, which determines whether staff fields go on the sheet at all.

**4. Access reality.** Whether the manager already has credentials from the sales
process, so the access block doesn't ask for things already in hand.

**5. Relationship constraints.** Anything about how this client should be handled — who
the actual decision maker is, whether the owner or an office manager will complete the
sheet, whether there's a spouse or partner who has to sign off. This changes the tone of
the covering message and sometimes the delivery channel.

## What does not qualify

- Anything the client can answer just as easily — that's what the sheet is for
- Anything discovery could have found with more effort — go find it
- Preferences about the sheet's format
- Confirmation of things already `verified`
- Anything where the honest answer is "the manager won't know either" — that's a blank,
  and blanks are fine

## How to ask

Batch all questions into one message. Attach a **proposed default** to each, so the
manager can accept most of them in one word and only engage with the ones they disagree
with. This mirrors what the sheet does to the client, and for the same reason.

```
Six things before I build the sheet. Defaults in brackets — tell me which
to override and I'll take the rest as-is.

1. Founding year — site says 1998, GBP says 2004, SC SOS registration says
   2011. [Default: put all three on the sheet and let them resolve it.]

2. Commercial work — still listed on four service pages. Did they mention
   dropping it? [Default: keep, mark as "still doing this?"]

3. Primary MSA — Hilton Head / Bluffton, or are we building for Savannah
   too? [Default: HHI-Bluffton only.]

...
```

If a tool for presenting tappable choices is available, use it — managers are often
answering from a phone between calls.

## After the interview

Update the record with the answers, marking them `manager_confirmed` with the date.
Anything the manager couldn't answer stays as-is and flows through to the client sheet.
Don't re-ask; a second round defeats the purpose.
